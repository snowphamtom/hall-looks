The Artist

Taylor Heller is an inventor and builder working at the intersection of engineering and visual art. Named inventor on eight patent applications spanning suspension systems, admission-control gates, and error-correction codes, he brings an engineer's discipline to AI-assisted image-making: every series is governed by a locked aesthetic system — composition rules, color hierarchies, and geometric constraints held constant across all variations.

Trains of Thought — his wire-tunnel masterpiece series — treats the train as a moving thought: chrome wire tunnels, striated iridescent walls, marbled floors, cities folded into rings and spinning against the clouds. Everything before this series, he says, was practice. Working iteratively with generative models, he art-directs each piece through dozens of generations, keeping only what obeys the lock.

His work has been built for the browser, the headset, and the gallery wall — always with the same method: define the lock, generate within it, keep only what survives.

The Story

The train is a thought that refused to stay still. It runs on rails of light through tunnels of chrome wire, and every station it passes is a dream it has already had.

Nothing here is solid. The walls are striations, the floor is marbled weather, the cities fold into rings and spin slowly against the clouds. The train travels one way — toward you, or left to right — the way a thought travels when it finally decides to arrive.

Seven pictures, one journey — arrival, the run, the vortex, the tilt, the pause, the ring, the synthesis. It should be what dreams dream: a machine for imagining, drawn at the speed of sleep.
