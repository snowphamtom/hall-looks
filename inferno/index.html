The Artist

Taylor Heller is an inventor and builder working at the intersection of engineering and visual art. Named inventor on eight patent applications spanning suspension systems, admission-control gates, and error-correction codes, he brings an engineer's discipline to AI-assisted image-making: every series is governed by a locked aesthetic system — composition rules, color hierarchies, and geometric constraints held constant across all variations.

The Inferno series renders Dante's Nine Circles as wireframe cathedrals, each circle a hallway of light drawn in a single color tier. Working iteratively with generative models, he art-directs each piece through dozens of generations, keeping only what obeys the lock. The result is architecture as moral geometry: ascent rendered in gold, descent in red and blue.

His work has been built for the browser, the headset, and the gallery wall — always with the same method: define the lock, generate within it, keep only what survives.

The Story

Dante's Inferno is a building. Nine circles, each deeper and narrower than the last — it was always architecture. I wanted to draw it the way an engineer would: as wireframes, load-bearing lines of light with nothing decorative holding them up.

Each circle gets one color, one hallway, one emotional temperature. Gold is the highest tier — the center everything ascends toward. Red is the entryway of the violent circles. Blue is the axis shadow, the cold structural truth underneath. Where tiers overlap, the seams blend to orange.

The compositions use the vesica piscis — overlapping circles with no hard edges — because damnation and salvation were never separate rooms, just overlapping fields. Every spiral rotates in the same direction, scaled by the golden ratio, with yellow as the master scale.

I generated hundreds of variants and kept only the ones that obeyed the lock: same spirals, same direction, same rotation, yellow edges at half opacity, no black ring around the gold center. What survived is the series.
